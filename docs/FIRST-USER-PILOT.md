# First-user pilot: can someone prepare for a real interview without help?

Start with 5–8 adult jobseekers, including people facing redundancy and people moving roles by choice. The goal is to learn whether the focused journey and documents are useful before adding features or connecting OpenAI.

## Invitation you can adapt

> I’m testing RoleBridge, a tool to help you find suitable jobs, check your experience against an advert and prepare for interviews. Would you try it for about 15 minutes on your own device and tell me what helped or confused you? You can use sample information. Your CV is read in your browser; RoleBridge does not upload it. Saving is optional, encrypted and limited to that browser. The interview pack includes sample answers to adapt, plus printable cheat sheets. This is an early version, with no AI connection or guarantee of interviews.
>
> Try it here: https://cmcgerty88.github.io/Ai-Jobs/

Before collecting feedback, give participants your real organiser name/contact, what feedback you will keep, why, who can see it, the deletion date and how to withdraw. See the existing [privacy review](PRIVACY-SECURITY-REVIEW.md#ukeu-gdpr-and-pilot-operations). Do not ask for their CV, passwords, backups, downloaded packs or private screenshots. Keep any recruitment contact list separate from coded feedback. A small assisted pilot on this hosting setup is different from a wider public launch; use a dedicated origin before expanding.

## Give one task, then watch where help is needed

“Use a job you might apply for, or an interview you have coming up. Find the route you need. Create an interview pack from your CV or manually entered examples, the full advert and any company/interview information you have. Open the full document and both cheat sheets. Pick one question and practise your answer.”

Let participants choose their route; do not explain every button first. They can enter a fictional example if they prefer. Do not record their screen. If you observe, ask them to keep private information off-screen and record navigation friction rather than personal content.

Check these outcomes with minimal yes/no or short coded notes:

- Did they choose an appropriate starting direction without help?
- Did CV extraction save time, and could they correct it?
- Did they understand which sample answers were fictional and which facts they must supply?
- Could they create, download and open the pack and two cheat sheets?
- Could they identify a concrete improvement to one interview answer?
- Did they understand whether their work would remain after closing the tab?

Do not infer success from a download alone. Ask them to describe the useful change in general terms without sharing their work history.

## Five questions afterwards

1. What did you expect the site to do when you first opened it?
2. Where did you hesitate or need help?
3. Which part of the pack helped you prepare a better answer? What was generic or missing?
4. Would you use this for another vacancy, and why?
5. What one change would make it worth recommending to someone else?

If they agree to a follow-up, ask a week later whether they actually used the pack or returned. Declared intention is not evidence of repeat use. Avoid collecting employer names, interview outcomes with identifiable details, or CV content in research notes. Set and follow a short deletion schedule, for example 30 days after analysing the pilot.

## Decide what to build next from the evidence

A useful early target is that at least 4 of the first 5 testers can create and open a pack without help, and at least 3 can describe a specific preparation benefit. These are practical pilot thresholds, not statistical proof of demand. Repeated confusion or inaccurate extraction takes priority over more features.

Potential next improvements, in order of the problem observed:

- **People get stuck:** simplify the exact step or improve extraction/review guidance.
- **Samples feel generic:** expand the relevant role examples and requirement prompts, then test them again.
- **People want more rehearsal:** add a short guided practice session and a self-review checklist for clarity, personal contribution and evidence, without pretending to score employability.
- **People return:** improve saved preparation and follow-up reminders before considering more tools or bringing back CV tailoring.

Keep OpenAI disconnected during this pilot. Revisit it only after feedback shows a clear need and the private backend, processing notice, minimised payload, retention/deletion, access controls and provider terms have been reviewed. Do not add keys to this public repository.
