# Test whether RoleBridge is useful

The next priority is to see whether a person can get one useful next action, return to record a result, and still find the tool useful. This release provides that journey without OpenAI or a server holding CVs. It does not yet validate demand.

## Your first run

1. Open https://cmcgerty88.github.io/Ai-Jobs/ and choose **Get my free Job Search Diagnosis**. No signup, payment or vacancy is required to get a first result.
2. Choose the situation closest to yours. Add a CV to draft your recent role, skills and work examples, or enter a recent role and one short example yourself. Review the draft and confirm only facts you can support. Imported draft fields stay out of encrypted saving and backups until reviewed. Remove unnecessary personal details.
3. Confirm the summary and choose **Show my next step**. Read the priority action and limitations. Can you explain what you will do next, and why? The small editorial library does not cover every career. Missing role suggestions are not a judgement about employability.
4. If you want to return, choose **Save my progress**. Use your own device and a unique password of 14–256 characters, including an uppercase letter and a special character. There is no reset. Otherwise continue without saving; refresh/close clears that session.
5. Optional: follow the priority action or open **More tools**. Review work preferences before relying on vacancy comparisons. Use Suggested vacancies to search LinkedIn, Indeed or CV-Library for your target or related titles. These links open external results; they do not import them. Add a vacancy manually when useful. Once the Reed connection is configured, try the in-app pool and add one matching listing to your shortlist. Until then the site clearly shows that the feed is unavailable; you can still add an advert yourself. Read the full advert, review the imported summary and leave missing facts unknown. See [vacancy-feed setup](VACANCY-FEED-SETUP.md). Build a tailored CV or interview pack only when useful. Downloads are unencrypted files you control; no ATS pass is guaranteed.
6. Record an application with **Progress & check-in**. Open **Update this application** for the chosen vacancy; record dates and completed interviews only. **Update outcome** commits the entry. Half-written vacancy and tracker forms are not saved; profile/preferences and committed records autosave only in an unlocked encrypted workspace.
7. Use **Backup, lock or delete** to lock when finished. Return in the same browser and unlock. The check-in asks what changed; update one application, or review your next step. Profile edits require confirmation before new role/application suggestions. A lack of reply is not automatically a rejection and the app cannot know the employer’s reason.
8. Optionally expand **Was this useful?** on the check-in page and preview the feedback summary. Nothing is sent. Share only if comfortable and after your pilot organiser explains how feedback will be used. Never share passwords, CVs or private screenshots through GitHub.

## Invite five people, then observe two sessions each

Use your existing recruitment relationships; do not scrape redundant workers or upload their contact details. Agree a participant notice and private contact route before collecting research data (see the privacy review). Let people stop without explanation. Do not record their private screen or ask them to send CVs.

In session one, ask the person to complete the journey with their own real job search. Note only: could they finish; where they needed help; did they identify one specific useful action; how long it took to reach the first useful result. Only test CV/interview packs if they are relevant to that person’s next action; do not require every participant to use every tool. If discussing a problem, record a short paraphrase without employer/person names. A yes/no usefulness answer alone is weak evidence.

A week later, ask whether they used that action, returned to update an outcome, and what they expected next. Ask what they use today, what they would miss if RoleBridge disappeared, and whether they would commit to a further paid trial at a clearly stated price. Do not take payments until terms, support and a real payment flow exist. Hypothetical willingness is weaker than an actual commitment.

Before inviting anyone, write down provisional success criteria. For five participants, a useful starting decision rule is: four complete the journey without substantial coaching, three identify and take an action, and three return voluntarily to update outcomes. These are product decision thresholds, not statistically representative findings or promised results. Track actual return behaviour with voluntary follow-up, not hidden analytics. Review failures as valuable evidence, not reasons to pressure people.

## Decide the next build from the evidence

- If onboarding is confusing, fix that before expanding features.
- If people cannot find appropriate real vacancies, assess an authorised job-data source with current UK/Irish coverage. The existing role library is editorial, not live listings.
- If users repeatedly need access on another device or account recovery, build hosted authentication and private database isolation using the security gates.
- If users can identify a repeated need for deeper analysis, revisit OpenAI only after privacy controls, provider terms and user-controlled processing are ready.

Keep the pilot small, record what actually happened, and do not claim the tool caused employment outcomes. Demand, usability and employment impact are different questions.
