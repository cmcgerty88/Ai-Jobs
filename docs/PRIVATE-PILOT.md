# Test whether RoleBridge is useful

The next priority is to see whether a person can get one useful next action, return to record a result, and still find the tool useful. This release provides that journey without OpenAI or a server holding CVs. It does not yet validate demand.

## Your first run

1. Open https://cmcgerty88.github.io/Ai-Jobs/ and look for **Private pilot · v0.2**. Refresh after deployment if the older version is shown.
2. Create a private workspace on your own device with a strong, unique passphrase. Keep it safe: there is no reset. Alternatively, choose Try without saving. No email or payment is required.
3. Read your CV locally or type your experience. Remove unnecessary personal details. Choose accurate examples and skills, say what help you want and what useful progress would look like, then confirm the profile.
4. Enter work preferences, explore target directions and save one or two worth checking.
5. Bring one actual vacancy from an employer/job site. Enter the facts, leave missing facts unknown and review the job analysis.
6. Open the dashboard. After applying, record the application date. Update outcomes later, including completed interviews. Add one next action and date. Dates/stages are committed using **Update outcome**; half-written vacancy and tracker forms are not saved. Profile/preferences and confirmed records autosave in an unlocked encrypted workspace.
7. Read the recommendation and its evidence limits. Correct inaccurate records. A lack of reply is not automatically a rejection; the app cannot know the employer's reason.
8. Lock when finished. Return in the same browser, unlock, reconfirm your profile and update your progress. The app remembers reviewed facts, goals, saved directions, job records and optional feedback. It does not retain the raw CV, observe your activity elsewhere, learn across users or train a model.
9. Optionally answer the two usefulness questions and preview the feedback summary. Nothing is sent. You decide whether to share that small summary directly with your pilot organiser after they explain how feedback will be used. Never share your passphrase or CV through GitHub.

## Invite five people, then observe two sessions each

Use your existing recruitment relationships; do not scrape redundant workers or upload their contact details. Agree a participant notice and private contact route before collecting research data (see the privacy review). Let people stop without explanation. Do not record their private screen or ask them to send CVs.

In session one, ask the person to complete the journey with their own real job search. Note only: could they finish; where they needed help; did they identify one specific useful action; how long it took. If discussing a problem, record a short paraphrase without employer/person names. A yes/no usefulness answer alone is weak evidence.

A week later, ask whether they used that action, returned to update an outcome, and what they expected next. Ask what they use today, what they would miss if RoleBridge disappeared, and whether they would commit to a further paid trial at a clearly stated price. Do not take payments until terms, support and a real payment flow exist. Hypothetical willingness is weaker than an actual commitment.

Before inviting anyone, write down provisional success criteria. For five participants, a useful starting decision rule is: four complete the journey without substantial coaching, three identify and take an action, and three return voluntarily to update outcomes. These are product decision thresholds, not statistically representative findings or promised results. Track actual return behaviour with voluntary follow-up, not hidden analytics. Review failures as valuable evidence, not reasons to pressure people.

## Decide the next build from the evidence

- If onboarding is confusing, fix that before expanding features.
- If people cannot find appropriate real vacancies, assess an authorised job-data source with current UK/Irish coverage. The existing role library is editorial, not live listings.
- If users repeatedly need access on another device or account recovery, build hosted authentication and private database isolation using the security gates.
- If users can identify a repeated need for deeper analysis, revisit OpenAI only after privacy controls, provider terms and user-controlled processing are ready.

Keep the pilot small, record what actually happened, and do not claim the tool caused employment outcomes. Demand, usability and employment impact are different questions.
